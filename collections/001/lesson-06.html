The real AHI milestone

On October 13, 2026, AZHomeInstalls completed its first paid job, sourced through Facebook Marketplace. The customer paid $200 cash in full. The work included mounting an 85-inch television using a customer-provided bracket, removing and boxing 15 pictures while leaving wall hooks in place, and hanging a portrait evenly between windows. A return visit for touch-ups was handled off the clock.

Separately, AHI configured a Stripe online payment link. The first-job cash payment and the availability of an online payment link are distinct facts; the latter did not process that first payment.

Core sequence: agree on scope and price → perform the work → verify money received → issue or record invoice/receipt accurately → reconcile to bookkeeping. Do not mark a job paid just because an invoice or link was sent.

Step 3 — Follow our Stripe Payment Link build

Use your own Stripe account, as AHI did. Open Stripe's current instructions, complete its required business setup, then create a hosted Payment Link for your service. The real AHI process established a business account and a working Payment Link; the first paid job was handled separately in cash.

Stripe's official Payment Links instructions ↗Open your own Stripe Dashboard ↗Check current Stripe processing costs ↗
  1. Open and control your own Stripe business account.
  2. Complete the onboarding details Stripe requests; available features depend on the account and region.
  3. In Stripe Dashboard, create a product or service price and a hosted Payment Link using the provider's current instructions.
  4. Review the customer-facing checkout details and copy the hosted link.
  5. Add the link to your business's payment workflow. Do not paste secret API keys into your website or LLM.
  6. Check payment activity in Stripe Dashboard rather than assuming a sent link means a customer has paid.

Step 4 — What the Stripe link does and does not do

A hosted Stripe Payment Link presents a checkout page and routes online payment through Stripe. Sending a link does not mean the payment succeeded. Confirm the transaction in your own Stripe Dashboard. AHI's first completed job was paid in cash and is separate from the Stripe implementation.

Step 5 — Set up real payments responsibly

For the AHI example, open your own Stripe account rather than allowing an instructor to own your funds. Start at official Stripe Payment Links documentation and follow current onboarding and identity/business verification instructions. Create the service Payment Link in your own Dashboard, test through Stripe-supported methods, and verify successful payments in your Stripe account before recording a transaction as paid. Check current terms, processing fees, dispute procedures and payout schedules directly with the provider. Use hosted checkout/payment links instead of collecting card details in your own HTML. Keep real customer names and payment identifiers out of public repositories, prompts and learning demos. Verify local tax and service-business requirements separately.

No live transaction takes place in this lesson. Following documentation or creating a link alone does not establish that a customer payment occurred. Test only through Stripe-supported testing methods.

Step 6 — Finish the Stripe lesson

  1. Generate your personalized Stripe Payment Links prompt and save it.
  2. Use the official Stripe documentation to establish and control your own account.
  3. Set up a service price and a hosted payment link, following the dashboard instructions.
  4. Check that the link displays the intended business, service and amount.
  5. Know how to verify an actual payment in Stripe, and how its fees are displayed.
  6. Retain ownership of the Stripe account, dashboard access, payout settings and transaction exports.

Pass the B.O.B. test: a beginner can follow the documented Stripe Payment Link process with their own account, understand what the link does, and control the resulting payment records. No new invoice or accounting software needs to be built to complete this lesson.
