Get paid. Keep your own records.

Learn how AZHomeInstalls accepted payment on its first job, established a Stripe payment link, and kept financial records. Use a fictional invoice exercise before handling real funds. An invoice is not proof of payment, and a payment link is not an invoice.

The real AHI milestone

On October 13, 2026, AZHomeInstalls completed its first paid job, sourced through Facebook Marketplace. The customer paid $200 cash in full. The work included mounting an 85-inch television using a customer-provided bracket, removing and boxing 15 pictures while leaving wall hooks in place, and hanging a portrait evenly between windows. A return visit for touch-ups was handled off the clock.

Separately, AHI configured a Stripe online payment link. The first-job cash payment and the availability of an online payment link are distinct facts; the latter did not process that first payment.

Core sequence: agree on scope and price → perform the work → verify money received → issue or record invoice/receipt accurately → reconcile to bookkeeping. Do not mark a job paid just because an invoice or link was sent.

Step 4 — Understand the records

  • Estimate: proposed work and price; no payment implied.
  • Invoice: request/record showing what is owed; sending does not confirm payment.
  • Payment link: processor-hosted way to pay; processing and payout may include fees.
  • Receipt: record of an actual payment, issued only once receipt is verified.
  • Reconciliation: match cash counted or processor settlement to the books. Track fees, partial payments, refunds, and disputes separately.

Step 5 — Set up real payments responsibly

For the AHI example, open your own Stripe account rather than allowing an instructor to own your funds. Start at official Stripe Payment Links documentation and follow current onboarding and identity/business verification instructions. Create the service Payment Link in your own Dashboard, test through Stripe-supported methods, and verify successful payments in your Stripe account before marking an invoice paid. Check current terms, processing fees, dispute procedures and payout schedules directly with the provider. Use hosted checkout/payment links instead of collecting card details in your own HTML. Keep real customer names and payment identifiers out of public repositories, prompts and learning demos. Verify local tax and service-business requirements separately.

No live transaction takes place in this lesson. The practice tool creates fictional documents and a CSV. Browser Print → Save as PDF may also be available. None of these activities charges a card, sends an invoice or verifies a real cash payment.

Step 6 — Finish the lesson

  1. Generate and save your LLM prompt and brief.
  2. Verify the full-payment and partial-payment examples.
  3. Export the fictional CSV and print/save the mock invoice.
  4. Write down the chosen payment method, provider fees, bookkeeping method, ownership and backup arrangements.
  5. Explain which event confirms funds were actually received.
  6. Keep the template and exports in storage and accounts you control.

Pass the B.O.B. test: a beginner can independently issue a clearly labeled sample invoice, reconcile the fictional example and update their own copy of the source. Live processing is a separate verified implementation.
