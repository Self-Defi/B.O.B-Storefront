The actual AHI case study

Outbound Automation System (OAS): AHI built an Arizona-focused prospect queue, Gmail API integration, follow-up rules, unsubscribe handling and CRM-connected outreach. Initial tests showed that email delivery does not guarantee inbox placement; Microsoft inbox testing exposed Junk placement, while a separate external mailbox later showed main-inbox placement. Follow-ups were intentionally excluded from the new-outbound daily cap.

Scheduling: AHI integrated Google Calendar availability and customer booking rules, with stated standard hours of 8:00 AM–4:00 PM, Monday–Sunday and an after-hours emergency premium. Screenshots demonstrate the configuration, not a guarantee that live booking is currently operating.

Important: Campaign queues, follow-up execution and authentication may change. Treat historical screenshots as evidence of development, not proof of current delivery reliability. The educational starter does not send mail or book calendar time.

Step 4 — Separate automation from proof

  • Queued: A message is waiting; nothing may have been attempted.
  • Sent: The provider accepted the send operation; it may still bounce.
  • Delivered: Delivery reporting indicates recipient-server acceptance, not primary inbox placement.
  • Inbox placement: Requires separate testing; not implied by sent or delivered status.
  • Follow-ups: Their queue is separate from the cap on new outbound messages, but opt-outs apply to both.
  • Calendar: Proposed slots are not available slots until validated with authorized live free/busy data.

Step 5 — Plan production responsibly

For a future AHI-style implementation, version-control code in your GitHub account; store contacts and campaign state in a secured database; process a durable queue with bounded retries; use provider-approved email authentication; enforce opt-outs; preserve audit logs; and authenticate calendar calls. Respect applicable marketing regulations and provider policies. Cloudflare Workers/D1 and Google Calendar can support parts of this architecture, but the demonstration does not create a secure production system.

Do not launch unsupervised bulk outreach from this lesson. Start with permission-appropriate recipients, a verified business sender, compliance review and controlled internal tests. No live contact data belongs in the demo.

Step 6 — Pass the B.O.B. philosophy test

  • Accessible: Another beginner can run the dummy-data exercise and explain the results.
  • Affordable: Sending service, domain, mailbox, database, hosting and insurance costs are identified separately; subscriptions are optional where technically feasible.
  • Independent: The builder owns code, recipient data, suppression records, provider accounts, recovery methods, exports and backups.
